# Self Love is the Canon of our Times
### A flagship workshop by Johnny Graf · Brave Plum Healing

**The promise (one line):** *A half-day to understand — in your body, not only your head — why self-love is the canon of our time, and to strike your own match: come as you are, and you may leave lit.*

> This is a workshop and a coaching practice — not therapy, counseling, or medical care, and not a substitute for any of them.

---

## The big idea

> "I believe the canon of our time is to awaken to self-love, and if we do so humanity is going to ascend to a height that we have not yet reached." — *Johnny, verbatim*

The whole argument in one breath: our gift is our light — *"and I believe the well from which we are supposed to dip for our self-admiration and self-love."* We were never told we have that well *"waiting for us 24/7/365,"* so we dip in other wells — we shop, we drink, we numb; we worship narcissistic leaders and stay chained to our devices. Self-love is not vanity and not a luxury. It is the strike that lets the torch burn:

> "How can we be a torch if we never strike the match?" — *Johnny, verbatim*

The individual's light and the healing of society are the same movement. *"once you figure out how to love yourself your well… resides within you"* — and only then is your gift, given to you on purpose, fully expressed for the humanity that needs it.

## The through-line — individual light → society

Four images carry the day, in order, and they double as the memory hooks:

**THE WELL** (your own light — the source you dip from) → **THE GIFT** (what your light is *for*, "given to you on purpose") → **THE TORCH / THE MATCH** (the light carried forward, from the one out to the many) → **THE CANON** (self-love as the defining work of our time; humanity's ascent).

We start wide — *why this matters for all of us* — and end close: *one real step that is yours alone.* The big idea earns the intimate turn. And this room exists for an honest reason, in Johnny's own words:

> "My journey to self love was long and arduous — several decades and plenty of scratches along the way. Some cuts too. Deep wounds. I'm curious if there is a way through that is less painful." — *Johnny, verbatim*

This workshop is that attempt at a less-painful way through.

---

## How this differs from — and feeds — The Well Within

**The Well Within already exists** (shipped, live-online small circle, hand mirror + journal, 90 minutes). It is the **HOW** — the intimate, ongoing *practice*: arrive → find your well → tending practices → name your gift → **the mirror** ("I love you, ____," carried home as a 30-day practice).

**Canon sits *above* it.** It is the **WHY** — the thesis/manifesto that reaches from the individual's own light *out* to society: the canon thesis, the two purposes of education, the repressed gift, the consequence cascade, the void-filling, stewardship-not-debt, and the incentive-shaped societal critique. Canon carries the whole *argument*; The Well Within carries the daily *practice*.

- **Canon = the sermon / the why.** Its signature is the *collective* match-striking — the individual light becoming a shared light.
- **The Well Within = the practice / the how.** Its signature is the *private* mirror — self to self.

Canon lets people *taste* the inward turn, but it deliberately **does not** re-teach or re-sell the 30-day mirror curriculum. It names it, tastes it, and points to it — an invitation, never a hard sell.

### 3 · The Two Purposes & The Repressed Gift  (30 min)

**Teaching beats**
- The two legitimate purposes, verbatim: education *"should either be enlightening the individual — letting them understand their light/magic — or it should provide society with some benefit. In a perfect world the enlightened human should serve the needs of humanity through sharing their gift to the world."* This is the hinge: the individual and society are one arc.
- The repression, verbatim: *"Public education has become a device that unfortunately represses our god-given gift. We are so busy assessing, cataloging, critiquing our children that they have exactly zero opportunity to explore their magic."* Universalize gently from school to the whole lifespan — most of us were *"programmed to believe that they are broken."*
- Johnny's own and only testimony, verbatim, summary level: *"There came a moment of realization that I was doing more harm than good."*
- The gift is on purpose, verbatim: *"your gift was given to you on purpose. It is no accident that you have this thing that lives inside of you waiting to be expressed."* And the assurance: *"There is no gift that is not worthy of sharing. Your gift is precious, and so is mine."*
- The reframe, offered as invitation not diagnosis: this waking-up is *"not a midlife crisis — a MIDLIFE TRANSFORMATION."*

**Participant practice** — *Name your gift, first draft.* Private card, 8–10 min: *"The thing that lives inside of me, waiting to be expressed, might be ______."* If naming it directly is too big, write instead what you lose track of time doing, or what people thank you for. Optional single sentence to the circle — *"My gift might be ______"* — pass always permitted.

*Facilitator note: Deliver the school critique as grief, not blame — many devoted teachers are trapped in the same device, and Johnny lived inside it. ### 4 · The Void & the Other Wells  (30 min)

**Teaching beats**
- The void-filling, verbatim: *"we chase each other around trying to find a new well to dip into, because we were never told that we have an abundant well waiting for us 24/7/365. But instead we find other ways to fill the void. We shop, we drink, we numb."*
- Name the consequence cascade **gently — strictly as the consequences of disconnection from one's gift, never as conditions this workshop treats or cures**: depression, addiction, low self-esteem, resentment, mental illness, violent ideation, broken relationships, burnout. Say plainly: *we are not here to fix these; we are here to name where the disconnection leads, and to turn toward the well.*
- The societal critique — incentive-shaped, NOT conspiratorial, verbatim: *"There is a reason why we are worshiping narcissistic leaders and bowing to the mega corporations while acquiescing our responsibilities to one another to influencers that keep us entertained and chained to our devices. I am not a conspiracy theorist by any means, but this is all working out for those in power and the ultra wealthy — seems quite Orwellian to me. And counter to what I believe we are meant to be."*
- The stakes, verbatim: *"self love is the defining moment of our time. In fact, I would go so far as saying that our lives depend on it. Because if we keep dipping into each other's wells… then our gifts will never be fully expressed."*

**Participant practice** — *My other wells (private inventory).* Each person lists, for their eyes only, the wells they dip into when the real one runs dry (the scroll, the cart, the drink, the comparison, the applause). No one collects these. Then one honest question written beside the list: *"What was I actually thirsty for?"* Close with a quiet prompt each answers only to themselves: *"What has that well cost me?"*

*Facilitator note: This is the sharpest place for the care guardrail — never say self-love treats, cures, or heals any cascade condition; keep the support line available and repeat it if the room goes heavy. Keep the critique incentive-shaped ("it works out for those in power"); if the room goes political, steer back to the invitation — the answer to a rigged well is not outrage, it is drinking from your own. This is the low point of the day's arc by design; the break and the turn are the lift.*

### 5 · The Turn — *from the world to my own well*  (30 min)

**Teaching beats**
- The pivot the whole day has built toward, verbatim: *"The love that I was intended to nurture was never for anyone else but myself. Because once you figure out how to love yourself your well — the place that you go to dip into energy and vitality resides within you."*
- The garden, verbatim: *"Nurturing a path of love in turn tends to our own garden. You flourish, and blossom, and create vitality in yourself. And once you learn how to 'garden' yourself, you will never forget."*
- Make the individual→society arc explicit again: tending your own well is not selfish — it is the *only* way the gift the world needs actually gets expressed. You are the site of the ascent.
- Name that this is a taste, not the whole practice: today we *find* the well; the daily tending is a practice you can take home.

**Participant practice** — *Locating your well* (guided stillness, 10–12 min, Johnny-led). Hand to the center of the chest, slow breath, attention turned inward: where in your body do you feel steadiest, most *here*? Let that place be the well — not somewhere to get to, somewhere that already is. Sit in silence for a full two minutes; close by silently offering yourself one kind sentence. Then one written line: *"What would it feel like to draw from my own well tomorrow morning?"*

*Facilitator note: This is a contemplative practice, not a clinical intervention — keep the language experiential ("notice," "let," "you might"). This must NOT become The Well Within's tending curriculum — no mirror, no 30-day protocol, no self-compassion teaching sequence here. One felt discovery, then stop. Some people will feel a lot; hold the silence, don't narrate over it. If the moment feels big for someone, coming back to the breath and a hand on the heart is a quiet, safe place to land — and the support resources named at the close are there for anything heavier.*

### 6 · Striking the Match — *THE SIGNATURE MOMENT*  (35 min)

**Teaching beats**
- Return the spine line, verbatim: *"How can we be a torch if we never strike the match?"* The whole day has been the argument; now the room does the thing.
- Stewardship, NOT debt — the gift is paid FORWARD, verbatim: *"Our gift lights us up, and in turn we carry our light forward through the dark of the universe."* You owe no one; you carry forward.
- Individual to society in one gesture: each person kindles their own light first (self-love, the match), and only then does the room become a field of torches — *"humanity is going to ascend to a height that we have not yet reached,"* rehearsed at human scale.
- Land it plainly: this small flame in your hands *is* the match, struck. The point was never the room; it is that you now know how to strike it again.

**Participant practice — THE STRIKING.** In a circle around one central *canon flame*, each person in turn: (1) says aloud one line — *"My gift is ______,"* or, if that is too much, simply *"I am striking my match"* — and (2) lights their own candle from the shared flame. Lights stay lit through the closing, and the room goes from dim to fully lit. Then everyone lifts their eyes to the shared light and sits with it in silence for a full minute, while the thesis is read once over the quiet: *"I believe the canon of our time is to awaken to self-love…"* **Online:** each participant lights their own candle on camera at their word, so the gallery of windows fills with flame together.

## The single signature moment

**The Striking of the Match.** Around one shared *canon flame*, each participant names their gift aloud (or simply says *"I am striking my match"*) and lights their own candle from the shared flame. A dim room fills with individual flames until it is fully lit — the manifesto made physical: one light, then many, then a lit room, the ascent rehearsed at human scale. It pays off the day's spine line — *"How can we be a torch if we never strike the match?"* — and it is deliberately, cleanly **distinct** from The Well Within's private hand-mirror *"I love you, ____"*: Canon's signature is *public, collective, outward* (the individual light becoming a shared light); The Well Within's is *intimate, mirrored, solo*. Online, every window lights its own candle together. (Dripless or LED/battery candles offered for safety; passing and silence always allowed.)
