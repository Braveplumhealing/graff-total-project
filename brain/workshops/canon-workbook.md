## How to use this workbook

Welcome. I'm glad you're here.

This is a workbook, which means it is not finished until you write in it. The teaching is short on purpose — a few plain words from me, and then real space that belongs to you. The blank lines are not decoration. They are the point. So keep a pen close, not a highlighter.

A few gentle ways to walk this:

**Go slow, and go in order.** There are four movements — **The Well**, **The Gift**, **The Torch**, and **The Canon** — and they build on each other, the way a day builds from morning to evening. You might do one movement a sitting. You might do the whole thing over a quiet weekend and come back to it in a season. There is no behind.

**There is a real pause built in,** about a third of the way through, where I'll ask you to close the book and get some water. Please take it. What comes after it lands softer on a rested body.

**There are no right answers, and no one is grading this.** Some prompts you'll fill with a paragraph. Some with a single word. Some you'll leave blank and come back to. All of that is right. A couple of these pages are for your eyes only, and I'll tell you which.

**A word about the language.** You'll notice I use words like *divine*, *the universe*, and *god* here and there. I reach for whichever one keeps all of us in the room — and I'd like you to do the same. Wherever one of those words lands wrong for you, cross it out and write the one that's honest in your own mouth, or none at all. This teaching does not belong to any single faith.

**One small thing about the shape.** Near the very beginning I'll ask you to write one word and finish one line. At the very end I'll ask you to answer them again — so the book closes where it opened. Don't skip the opening. It's half of a pair.

And please read the short word of care on the next page before you begin.

*— Johnny*

## A plain word of care

Before we start, one honest note — the same one I'd say if we were sitting together.

**This is a workbook and a coaching practice — not therapy, counseling, or medical care, and not a substitute for any of them.** It makes no medical or clinical claims, and it does not treat, cure, or heal any condition.

Somewhere in these pages we'll name, gently, where a long disconnection from your own gift and your own well can lead. I name those things only as the far end of what happens when we go too long dipping in other wells — never as things this workbook fixes. We are not here to fix them. We are here to name where the disconnection leads, and to turn toward the well.

## Before we begin · Arrival at the precipice

Come in. Sit down. There's nothing to prepare and nothing to prove. You made it to the table. That already counts.

Here is the simple philosophy this whole book grows out of. It's the one I carry on my walk around the block:

> "we come from love, we return to love, and most importantly that our work while on our earthly journey is to nurture a path of love."
>
> *— Johnny*

Hold that lightly for now. By the end, I hope you'll feel the surprise hidden inside it — the part about *whose* love it was really for all along.

I'll be honest about why this book exists at all. My own road here was not short:

> "My journey to self love was long and arduous — several decades and plenty of scratches along the way. Some cuts too. Deep wounds. I'm curious if there is a way through that is less painful."
>
> *— Johnny*

This workbook is my attempt at that less-painful way through. My decades, made into a map, so you might bleed a little less than I did.

And I believe we're doing this at an important moment — I'll say it plainly, not to alarm you, just to be honest about where we're standing:

> "we stand on a precipice, the last edge of the not knowing, and the beginning of an awakening that we have not yet experienced in our humanity. I believe that very soon many of us are going to look at one another and ask the question, 'Why did we hate each other'."
>
> *— Johnny*

This small, private work at your kitchen table is not separate from that big question. It's where the answer starts.

### Arrive, and plant two seeds

**One breath first.** Set the pen down. Put one hand on the center of your chest — the place we'll be talking about the whole way through. Take one slow breath, all the way in, all the way out. That's the whole practice for now. When you're ready, pick the pen back up.

**Now write one word** — just one — for what you carried in the door with you today. Not a sentence, not an explanation. The first honest word:

### The other wells

Here's what happens when no one ever tells you that you have a well of your own:

> "we chase each other around trying to find a new well to dip into, because we were never told that we have an abundant well waiting for us 24/7/365. But instead we find other ways to fill the void. We shop, we drink, we numb."
>
> *— Johnny*

You know these other wells. The scroll. The cart. The drink. The applause. The comparison. The extra hour of work. None of them are evil — they're just not yours, and they never fill. You dip, and an hour later you're thirsty again.

And when a person stays cut off from their own light for long enough, it costs them. I'll name where that can lead — gently, and let me be very clear about what I'm doing: depression, addiction, low self-esteem, resentment, broken relationships, burnout. **We are not here to fix or cure any of those, and this workbook does not treat, heal, or fix them.** I name them only as the far end of a long disconnection — so that we can turn, together, back toward the well.

### A real breath — set the book down

Stop here.

We've just named the dark, and the body needs a beat before the turn. This isn't a page to read past. It's a page that asks you to close the book.

So close it. Get a glass of water. Walk to the window, or around the block if you can. Feel your feet. Let your shoulders come down from around your ears. What comes next is the whole reason for the book, and it lands better after real rest, not after pushing through.

There's no timer. Come back in ten minutes, or tomorrow morning with your coffee. The book will keep.

### The turn — from the world back to your own well

Welcome back.

Here is the pivot the whole movement has been building toward. It took me years and more than one wrong guess to find it. I tried loving every human I met, and I ended up exhausted and resentful. I tried loving harder — same result. The puzzle only unlocked when I understood this:

> "The love that I was intended to nurture was never for anyone else but myself. Because once you figure out how to love yourself your well — the place that you go to dip into energy and vitality resides within you."
>
> *— Johnny*

Read that twice. All that talk at the start about nurturing a path of love? The love to nurture, first, was your own — not instead of others, but so there'd be something real to give them. And when you do:

> "Nurturing a path of love in turn tends to our own garden. You flourish, and blossom, and create vitality in yourself. And once you learn how to 'garden' yourself, you will never forget."
>
> *— Johnny*

So let's settle something, in case a small voice is calling this selfish. It is the opposite. Tending your own well is the only way the gift the world actually needs ever gets expressed. You are not stepping away from the world by turning inward. You are becoming the place where the world's healing can begin.

What follows is a taste — one felt discovery, not the whole daily practice. Today we *find* the well. The daily tending is something you can carry home, and I'll point you to where it lives.

**Practice — locating your well.**

Put one hand on the center of your chest again. Slow your breath, three or four times, and turn your attention gently inward. Where in your body do you feel steadiest — most *here*, most yourself? Don't hunt for it. Just notice where the ground is. Sit with it, without forcing an answer, for two slow minutes. Let that steady place be your well — not somewhere to get to, somewhere that already is.

## Movement Two · THE GIFT
### What your light is *for* — given to you on purpose

So if we each carry a well and a light, why do so few of us know it? Part of the answer is how we were raised and schooled. I spent years as a school principal, and I left. There's no soft way to say why, so here are my own words for it:

> "There came a moment of realization that I was doing more harm than good."
>
> *— Johnny*

I don't say that to blame the good, tired, devoted people inside our schools — most of them are trapped in the same machine, and I was one of them. I say it because I came to believe our work with one another should be doing one of two things, in my words:

> "education should either be enlightening the individual — letting them understand their light/magic — or it should provide society with some benefit. In a perfect world the enlightened human should serve the needs of humanity through sharing their gift to the world."
>
> *— Johnny*

See how those are one arc, not two? The individual's light and the good of everyone else are the same movement. But look what we built instead:

> "Public education has become a device that unfortunately represses our god-given gift. We are so busy assessing, cataloging, critiquing our children that they have exactly zero opportunity to explore their magic."
>
> *— Johnny*

And it doesn't stop at graduation. Most of us go on being measured and corrected for a lifetime, until we're half-convinced we're broken — *"programmed to believe that they are broken,"* in my words. We spend decades managing our weaknesses and hiding our magic.

But here is the truth the machine couldn't erase:

> "your gift was given to you on purpose. It is no accident that you have this thing that lives inside of you waiting to be expressed."
>
> *— Johnny*

And because you might be waiting for permission to believe it:

> "There is no gift that is not worthy of sharing. Your gift is precious, and so is mine."
>
> *— Johnny*

If you feel this stirring in you now, in the middle of your life, I want to gently reframe it. This is not a midlife crisis. It's a midlife transformation.

## Movement Three · THE TORCH / THE MATCH
### The light carried forward — one out to many

This is the moment the whole walk has been the argument for. Here's the spine line, and this time it isn't a question to think about. It's a thing to do:

> "How can we be a torch if we never strike the match?"
>
> *— Johnny*

You found your well. You named your gift. Now you strike your match.

And before you do, let me take one weight off your shoulders. Your gift is not a debt. You don't owe anyone a repayment for being lit up. It runs the other direction:

> "Our gift lights us up, and in turn we carry our light forward through the dark of the universe."
>
> *— Johnny*

It's paid forward, never back. You carry the light on — that's all. One light, struck, becomes many. That is how a dim room fills with flame. That is humanity ascending to a height we have not yet reached, happening at the smallest possible scale: you, at your table, striking one match.

In the live training, we do this together — a room of people, one shared flame, each person lighting their own candle until the whole room glows. You're doing it alone at your kitchen table, and it counts every bit as much. The room was never the point. The point is that you now know how to strike it — again and again, for the rest of your life.

### The signature practice · Strike your own match

*This is the heart of the workbook. Give it room. Don't rush up to it, and don't crowd the page around it.*

**1. Make it quiet.** Set the book down for a moment. One hand on your chest, one slow breath, the way you began.

**2. Optional — light a flame.** If you'd like to make it physical, light a candle. A real candle *or* a battery / LED candle — completely equal, the light is the point, not the fire. Open flame is never required. *A plain fire-safety word first, and it comes before the strike:* if you use a real flame, set it on a stable surface, keep it clear of anything that can catch, keep water nearby, and never leave it burning unattended.

**3. Strike it — in writing, and out loud if you can.** Write one of these two lines, whichever you can reach today. Write it as large as you like:

> My gift is __________________________________

— or, if naming the gift still feels like too much to claim today, simply, and mean it:

> I am striking my match.

Either one is a full, true strike. Passing on the bigger sentence is always allowed.

**4. Read the thesis once, slowly, over the quiet you've made** — out loud if you can:

> "I believe the canon of our time is to awaken to self-love, and if we do so humanity is going to ascend to a height that we have not yet reached."
>
> *— Johnny*

**5. Sit with it for a full minute.** Let the candle burn, or the ink dry. Don't rush off the moment — let it run a little longer than feels comfortable. That extra beat is where it lands.

## Movement Four · THE CANON
### Self-love as the defining work of our time

Lift your eyes for a moment. You just did a small, private thing at a kitchen table. Now let me show you what it's part of.

> "I believe the canon of our time is to awaken to self-love, and if we do so humanity is going to ascend to a height that we have not yet reached."
>
> *— Johnny*

When I say *canon*, I don't mean rules handed down to you. I mean the central work of an age — the one thing that, if we get it right, changes everything downstream of it. Every era has its canon. I believe ours is this: to awaken to self-love. Not vanity. Not a spa day. The real thing — learning to be the source of your own worth, drawing from your own well.

And here is the bigger current under all our other wells. I want to say it carefully, because it is not a conspiracy — it's a matter of incentives:

> "There is a reason why we are worshiping narcissistic leaders and bowing to the mega corporations while acquiescing our responsibilities to one another to influencers that keep us entertained and chained to our devices. I am not a conspiracy theorist by any means, but this is all working out for those in power and the ultra wealthy — seems quite Orwellian to me. And counter to what I believe we are meant to be."
>
> *— Johnny*

I don't tell you that to make you angry at the world. The answer to a rigged well was never outrage. It's learning to drink from your own. Which is exactly why I believe the stakes are as high as they are:

> "In this way, self love is the defining moment of our time. In fact, I would go so far as saying that our lives depend on it. Because if we keep dipping into each other's wells without understanding that we carry the divine within us through our gifts then our gifts will never be fully expressed."
>
> *— Johnny*

That precipice I named at the start — the edge where soon we look at one another and ask *"Why did we hate each other"* — this is how we step back from it. Not all at once, and not out there. Right here. One person remembering they have a well, and striking one match. That is the canon, beginning at human scale. It begins with you.

## Carrying the light forward · The close

Look at what just happened. You arrived carrying a word. You found a well that was yours all along. You named your gift, and you struck your own match. That is the argument of this whole book, made real in your own handwriting — and it's no small thing. I want you to feel it.

Now let me be honest with you, the way I'd want someone to be honest with me. A match struck once must be tended, or it goes out. My own road was long and arduous precisely because I kept striking and forgetting, striking and forgetting. The less-painful way through isn't a single afternoon at a table. It's a practice.

There's a destination I hold for this work — a moment I'd love for you to reach: to look yourself in the mirror and say, *"I love you, ____."* Not as a performance. As a plain, true thing. I won't pretend one workbook page gets you all the way there. What I'll offer is the very first taste of it — the beginning of a practice, not its completion.
