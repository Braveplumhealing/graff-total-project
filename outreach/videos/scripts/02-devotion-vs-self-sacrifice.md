# Script 02 — Devotion vs. Self-Sacrifice

**Runtime:** ~3:00 at an unhurried pace · **Format:** direct to camera, one take
**The lead (Johnny's chosen line, verbatim):** "Devotion serves that which we love —
self-sacrifice tries to prove that we are worthy of love."
**Ground rule for this script:** zero autobiography. The teaching stands on its own legs;
every example belongs to the listener. (Source conversation vaulted in
brain/downloads/2026-08-25-devotion-and-self-sacrifice.md — NOT YET.)

---

*[Look into the lens. Still. Let the first line land before anything else — no hello,
no wind-up. This one opens cold.]*

Devotion serves that which we love.

Self-sacrifice tries to prove that we are worthy of love.

*[beat — let it sit]*

I want to walk around that one with you,
because from the outside, those two look identical.

The long hours look the same.
The saying yes looks the same.
The showing up early, the staying late,
the carrying more than your share —
same clothes, same face.

You cannot tell them apart by watching the giving.

You can only tell them apart by what happens to the **giver**.

*[beat]*

Devotion includes you.
Self-sacrifice erases you.

Devotion says: *this matters to me, and I choose to offer myself to it.*

Self-sacrifice says something quieter, underneath:
*for this to matter… I have to disappear.*

*[beat]*

Maybe you know somebody like this.
Maybe you *are* somebody like this.

The one who never takes the vacation days.
The one who answers the email at nine at night.
The parent who hasn't done one thing for themselves in years
and wears it like a medal.
The team member everyone calls *the rock* —
and nobody's ever once asked how the rock is doing.

Here's how you can tell which side of the line you're on.

Devotion can say **no** — and keep right on loving.
Self-sacrifice hears its own *no* and calls it betrayal.

Devotion can rest.
Self-sacrifice rests and feels **guilty**.

Devotion can *receive* —
a thank-you, a gift, a hand.
Self-sacrifice only knows how to **earn**.

*[beat]*

And underneath all that earning,
there's usually a bargain nobody ever said out loud:

*If I give enough, they'll value me.*
*If I carry everything, they can't let me go.*

But here's the hard truth about that bargain:
the people and places we make it with
never signed it.
They receive the labor —
and they never knew about the contract
your heart attached to it.

*[beat — soften]*

Now — I'm not telling you to give less.
Every meaningful life costs something.
Love that costs nothing usually *is* nothing.

The question was never *"should I sacrifice?"*

The question is:
**does this giving come from my wholeness —
or does it require me to abandon it?**

And if you want to take just one thing around the block today,
take these two questions.
Ask them before the next big yes:

*What is **not** mine to give?*

*And who **else** pays the price when I give it?*

Because your time is never only yours.
Somebody's waiting at home for the part of you
that got left at work.

*[beat]*

Self-sacrifice says: *I will leave myself to serve you.*

Devotion says: *I will remain fully myself — and stand beside you.*

Be devoted.
**Stay whole.**
Turns out you can do both.

I'm Johnny.
This is Brave Plum Healing.
And when you're ready — the door's open at **braveplumhealing.com**.

No pressure, ever.
